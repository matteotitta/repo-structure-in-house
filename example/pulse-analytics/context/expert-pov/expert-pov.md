# PulseAnalytics — expert POV + OBI (canonical, locked example)

> **Note on this file:** PulseAnalytics is a fictional B2B SaaS analytics platform. The founder below — Maya, a former VP Marketing who built the product after living the problem — is illustrative. The POV is generic enough to apply across SaaS, specific enough to feel real. **Replace this file with your own founder's POV.** Keep the structure; replace the content.

## Belief map

Core beliefs, the assumption under each, and where each invites push-back:

- **"Attribution's job is to end the Monday argument, not produce a prettier chart."**
  Assumption: the value of measurement is a decision, not a dashboard. Push-back: data teams argue the model rigor matters more than the meeting. Maya's answer: rigor that no operator can act on is academic.
- **"If a VP Marketing needs a data team to answer 'what drove pipeline,' the tooling failed."**
  Assumption: the operator closest to the question should be able to answer it. Push-back: "some questions genuinely need a warehouse." Maya: most don't; the warehouse is how vendors hide a 2-week answer behind a 2-quarter project.
- **"Last-touch isn't wrong because it's inaccurate. It's wrong because it ends conversations that should keep going."**
  Assumption: attribution models are conversation tools, not truth machines.

## Contrarian positions (hot takes)

- **"Most 'data-driven marketing' is data-decorated instinct."** Consensus: more dashboards = more rigor. Maya: teams collect metrics they never make a decision with, then call the instinct-based call "data-driven" after the fact.
- **"The marketing data team is usually a symptom, not a solution."** Consensus: hire data people to fix attribution. Maya: you hired them because your tools require an engineer — fix the tools.
- **"Self-serve product analytics taught marketers to track everything and decide nothing."**

## Origin stories

- **The board deck that broke her.** As VP Marketing, Maya spent a Sunday stitching Salesforce + GA4 for a Monday board review, got asked one question her stitched report couldn't answer, and watched a quarter of good work get discounted in real time. "The work was fine. The proof was unbuildable in the time I had."
- **The Looker quote.** A vendor scoped a "proper" attribution build at one engineering quarter. "I realized I was being sold a delay, not a solution." That gap — 2 weeks vs 2 quarters — became the product.

## Taste profile

- **Admires:** tools an operator can run alone; writing that names the painful ritual instead of the abstract benefit; vendors who say what they're *not* for.
- **Rejects:** "synergy"-grade copy; dashboards-as-deliverables; attribution culture wars; demos on sandbox data.
- **Quality bar:** could a VP Marketing with zero engineering support stand this up and run it? If no, it's not done.

## Theme clusters

1. The Monday review as the real job-to-be-done (not "analytics")
2. Why the marketing data team is a tooling failure
3. Attribution as a conversation tool, not a truth machine
4. Operator-owned tooling vs engineering-gated tooling
